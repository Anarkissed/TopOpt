AND IT COULD NOT HAVE BEEN OTHERWISE — a structural fact, not a coincidence
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
A "Keep clear" clearance CANNOT remove part material. `rasterize_clearance`
(core/src/voxel/clearance.cpp:229) skips every voxel that maps back to a solid
imported-part voxel:

    // PRECEDENCE: never void PART material. A solved voxel mapping to a
    // solid part voxel is FrozenSolid/keep-in and wins — skip it.

Measured, on this same job with the same bore opened up until it forbids 80%
of the whole domain:

The allowed-voxel count does not move with the first three because every extra
frozen voxel is in the design box's EMPTY growth region — none of them is part
material. The last row does move the marginality reading (311 -> 252 voxels,
step 1 -> step 8), which is the metric responding correctly; the verdict still
does not, because the imported bracket itself carries a path from anchor to
load and no clearance can touch it.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE GUARD'S REACH. On a declared-load-case job the
pre-flight can only refuse when the IMPORTED PART is itself disconnected
between its anchor faces and its load faces (a multi-body import, a repair that
split the mesh), or when the design domain is configured so the two never meet.
A keep-clear cannot cause it, and neither can a keep_out box (voxel.hpp: "a
keep-out box never carves into the part regardless of freeze_part"). The guard
is therefore narrower in reach than the ten-hour run suggested — and it is
still the right belt to hold, because it is the only thing standing between a
disconnected import and a ladder that spends hours discovering it, and because
it costs 23 ms.
