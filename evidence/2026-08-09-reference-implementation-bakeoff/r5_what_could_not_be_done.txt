R5 — WHAT COULD NOT BE DONE, AND WHAT BROKE
============================================
Task 2026-08-09-reference-implementation-bakeoff, bar R5: "REPORT WHAT YOU COULD
NOT DO. A build that failed, a dependency that would not compile on Apple
Silicon, a constraint that could not be expressed — each is a finding and
belongs in the handoff at full size, not in a footnote."

Seven. Two are about scale, three are about the library's own robustness, one is
a constraint that has no expression, and one is a defect of this task's own.


1. ★ HIS FOUR-RUNG LADDER CANNOT BE RUN FROM SCRATCH ON HIS MAC. (the big one)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MEASURED, not extrapolated from a guess: 4 ALM iterations on his grid
(1,473,696 displacement DOFs, matrix 1,452,696 x 1,452,696 with 113,893,146
nonzeros) took 1110.922 s of loop time — **277.7 s per ALM iteration** — plus
~130 s of setup. Serial, PETSc GAMG-preconditioned CG, their own solver options
verbatim. `s2_from_scratch_timing.log` has the run.

Against SIMP on the same grid and the same job: 447 outer iterations in
4988.398 s = **11.2 s per iteration**. GridapTopOpt costs **24.9x more wall per
iteration**.

An augmented-Lagrangian level-set run needs its volume constraint to come home,
and at iteration 3 of this one it was at Vol = 0.309 and moving by ~0.003 per
iteration. Hundreds of iterations is the realistic figure, and their own
`AugmentedLagrangian` defaults to `maxiter = 1000`. At 277.7 s that is **21 to
77 hours for ONE rung**, and his ladder has four.

WHAT WAS NOT TRIED, and would be the first thing to try next:
  * MPI. Their headline script is the MPI one and the library is built for it;
    this task ran SERIAL. A 6-rank run on this 10-core machine might give 4-5x,
    which would bring one rung inside a working day but not four.
  * `-ksp_rtol 1.0e-12`, which is their example's setting and is very tight for
    a shape-derivative computation. Loosening it is free and untested here.
  * Reusing the GAMG hierarchy between ALM iterations.
None of these changes the order of magnitude, and none was measured, so none is
claimed.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THIS TASK: the from-scratch arm is reported at 3 iterations,
UNCONVERGED and labelled as such in every table (`GTO-ALM-3iter-UNCONV`), and
the four-rung geometry answer comes from the sequential arm instead (§S2.6).


2. ★ `update_labels!` IS O(n²) AND DOES NOT RETURN AT HIS SIZE.
-----------------------------------------------------------------
GridapTopOpt's `update_labels!` is how the reference scripts declare a Dirichlet
region. Its 3-D branch promotes the marked vertices to edges, faces and cells
with (Utilities.jl:130-140)

    edge_entries = [findall(x -> any(x .∈ vtx_edge_connectivity[1:end .!= j]),
                            vtx_edge_connectivity[j]) for j = 1:n]

— quadratic in the number of MARKED VERTICES, and it allocates a fresh copy of
the whole connectivity array once per vertex. Their examples tag one face of a
cube, where n is a few hundred. This problem marks 396,797 of his 491,232 nodes
(everything the extension velocity must not move). The call did not return: a
full-resolution run sat at 100% of one core for over twenty minutes without
reaching iteration 0.

REPLACED, and the replacement PROVED EQUIVALENT. `tag_vertices!` in
`his_part_ALM.jl` does the VERTEX half and skips the edge/face/cell promotion,
which is not an approximation for what it is used for: at order 1 every
Lagrangian dof sits on a vertex, so the Dirichlet dof set is decided by the
vertex entities alone. `verify_labelling.jl` builds the same three tags BOTH ways
on his problem at coarsen 4, where theirs is affordable, and compares the FE
spaces that come out — free dofs, Dirichlet dofs, and the free-dof-to-node maps
themselves. `s2_labelling_control.txt` is the result: IDENTICAL, and 239.8x
faster at coarsen 4 alone (the quadratic term is ~3,700x worse again at full
resolution).


3. ★ TWO OVERLAPPING `update_labels!` CALLS SILENTLY ERASE EACH OTHER.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
`_update_labels_locally!` writes ONE entity per vertex:
`labels.d_to_dface_to_entity[1][vtxs_Γ] .= entity`. Two calls whose masks overlap
do not union — the second overwrites the first. Every anchor node on his part is
inside the frozen anchor pad and therefore also in the velocity-pin set, so
tagging anchor-then-pinned left "Gamma_D" with **zero** Dirichlet DOFs, and the
run went ahead and solved an unclamped structure without complaining.

Caught by printing `num_dirichlet_dofs(V)` and seeing 0 where 21,000 was
expected. Fixed with three DISJOINT entities recomposed by
`add_tag_from_tags!`. The printed clamped-DOF count is now a standing check in
the driver, and at full resolution it reads 21000 — core's own number.


4. ★ THEIR REINITIALISER RETURNS NaN, AND `H_η` SWALLOWS IT.
--------------------------------------------------------------
`FiniteDifferenceReinitialiser` (first-order upwind eikonal) returns NaN at a
handful of nodes for several perfectly reasonable initial level sets on his
geometry. What makes it hard to diagnose is the second half:

    function H_η(t,η)
      if     t < -η   return zero(M)
      elseif abs(t)<=η return 1/2*(1+t/η+1/pi*sin(pi*t/η))
      elseif t > η    return one(M)
      end
    end

NaN matches none of the three branches, so the function falls off the end and
returns **`nothing`**. The run then dies far away with
`MethodError: no method matching -(::Int64, ::Nothing)` inside Gridap's assembly,
with nothing in the message pointing at the level set.

MEASURED on his own part at full resolution (`diag_scale.jl`, `diag_edt.jl`):

    initial field                                   NaN nodes (of 491,232)
    their `initial_lsf(4,0.2)`, unscaled                     0
    the same scaled by 4h                                    1
    6-neighbour BFS distance over the whole part          6767
    6-neighbour BFS + their seeding, band 5                  1
    6-neighbour BFS + their seeding, band 2                  0
    EXACT Euclidean distance over the whole part             0
    exact EDT + their seeding                                1
    part-envelope EDT + their seeding scaled to h            0   <- used

THE CAUSE, and it is ours as much as theirs: a 6-neighbour BFS distance is an L1
distance. Its gradient is wrong by up to 73% on diagonals and is DISCONTINUOUS,
and a first-order upwind eikonal solver assumes |∇φ| ≈ 1. Replacing it with an
exact Euclidean distance transform (Felzenszwalb & Huttenlocher's separable
lower-envelope algorithm, `edt.jl`) takes 6767 NaN nodes to 0. Isolated one-cell
islands from a thresholded hole seeding are the remaining fragility.

★ THIS IS EXACTLY THE FAILURE MODE THE TASK EXISTS TO AVOID, arriving from the
other direction: a bad initial field of OURS would have looked identical to "the
method does not work on his part". It is written down here so the next reader
does not have to rediscover it.


5. ★ `initial_lsf` ASSUMES A UNIT-SIZED DOMAIN AND ALIASES ON HIS PART.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
`initial_lsf(ξ,a)` is `-1/4 ∏cos(ξπxᵢ) - a/4`, so its holes repeat with period
2/ξ IN MODEL UNITS. Their examples run on a 2x1x1 domain where ξ=4 puts four
holes across it. His part is 218 mm across: ξ=4 there is a **0.5 mm period, four
times finer than his 1.705 mm voxel**. The reinitialiser collapses the aliased
field to all-solid (max φ exactly 0.000, `diag_scale.jl`). ξ = 2/(8h) — a hole
every 8 voxels — is what this task uses.

Nothing in the function's docstring says its argument is in domain units. It is
not a bug; it is an undocumented assumption that costs an afternoon.


6. ★ THE PART EXTERIOR CANNOT BE REMOVED FROM THE FEA, AND THAT IS STRUCTURAL.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
core marks the 357,320 non-part voxels FrozenVoid and they contribute no element.
A level-set method on a Cartesian background cannot: the level set is defined,
evolved and reinitialised over the whole box, and the operator has to stay
non-singular there. The GridapTopOpt arm therefore carries ϵ = 1e-3 of PLA's
modulus across 76.31% of the box.

The consequence was not hypothetical. WITHOUT a part mask on the stiffness the
optimiser grows structure OUTSIDE his part and collects FULL modulus for it:
8.4% of the part's volume had moved outside the part by iteration 20 of a
coarse run and was still climbing. With the mask (exterior pinned at the ersatz
floor whatever φ does) it settles at 1.9-2.6%. It never reaches zero, because
nothing in the method holds a boundary onto an imported envelope.

The fix that WOULD be structural — cutting the background mesh to the part — is
the Unfitted/CutFEM path, which needs a conforming surface mesh of his part to
cut with and is a different set of examples. Not attempted; §S2.7 says what it
would take.


7. THE CELL-SIZE TRANSITION QUESTION IS ONLY HALF-ANSWERED.
------------------------------------------------------------
S3(b) asks what happens at the transition between two cell sizes — PR 302
measured a seam there. LatticeLibrary ships exactly two `ICellArray`
implementations, `RegularCellArray` and `ConformalCellArray`, and NEITHER grades
the cell size spatially. So this task measured what it could: three SEPARATE
uniform cell sizes (2.0, 4.6026, 8.0 mm) and the continuity of the THICKNESS
grading within each. A graded-cell-size array would have to be written, which
would make it a build task rather than a measurement — the task's own
BLOCKED-STOP condition — and it is left as one.


AND ONE DEFECT OF THIS TASK'S OWN, since it wasted a run
---------------------------------------------------------
`design_rung_dump.cpp` used `char stem[128]` for an output path. The session
scratch directory is ~140 characters, so `snprintf` truncated the PATH, reported
success, and wrote four files into a directory that was not the one asked for.
The next program failed with "No such file or directory" on a path that looked
right. Fixed to `std::string`, with a comment saying why.
