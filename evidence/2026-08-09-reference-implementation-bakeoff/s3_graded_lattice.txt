S3.b — DOES THE THICKNESS VARY CONTINUOUSLY?
---------------------------------------------
★ THE MECHANISM IS CONTINUOUS BY CONSTRUCTION, AND IT IS TWENTY LINES.
LatticeLibrary's `IBeamThickness` is a single method

    float fGetBeamThickness(Vector3 vecPt)

— a thickness at a POINT, not per cell. `FieldBeamThickness` in the program is a
trilinear read of core's own per-voxel field at that point, which is literally
what the maintainers prescribe in PicoGK discussion #29 ("look up the simulation
value at the relevant point in space... use the value you read from the position
to modulate the wall thickness"). `PicoGK.Lattice.AddBeam(a, b, radiusA, radiusB)`
then TAPERS each strut between its two end radii, and the assembly function
queries the thickness `nSubSample` times along every beam (5 here, their default
is 2). There is no per-cell quantisation anywhere in the path, so there is no
cell-to-cell seam to measure. ★ PR 302's seam question does not arise for
THICKNESS grading in this kernel.

★ BUT THE FIELD HE HAS TO DRIVE IT WITH IS BINARY, AND THAT IS WHAT LIMITS IT.
Sampled 2000 times along the part's x and z mid-lines, the largest thickness
STEP between adjacent samples ~0.1 mm apart, as a percentage of the full
0.45-2.4 mm thickness range:

    driver                              along x     along z
    core's DENSITY field (rung 068)      18.69%      23.33%
    core's VON MISES field               15.80%       9.63%

A continuous grading over a 0.1 mm step should move the thickness by well under
1%. It moves by 10-23% because the DENSITY FIELD IS BINARY — PR 315 measured
96.6% of his boundary voxels sitting at one end of the band or the other — so
`thickness(rho)` jumps the full range across one voxel however smooth the
function is. The von Mises field is better (it has real interior variation) and
still steps, because it too falls to zero outside the solid.

★ THIS IS PR 315'S FINDING ARRIVING FROM THE LATTICE SIDE. The tool can grade
continuously; the field cannot ask it to. Whatever drives a graded lattice has to
carry spatial variation the density field does not have — which is exactly why
core's own `grade_lattice` takes a DEMAND field and not the density.

CELL SIZE, three uniform arrays (von Mises driver, PicoGK voxel = his design
voxel for this comparison only):

    cell      cells      lookups      generate   fill of the design   peak managed
    2.00 mm   311,080    19,909,120    36.5 s          85.38%            1,614 MB
    4.60 mm    28,665     1,834,560     4.0 s          35.62%              267 MB
    8.00 mm     6,032       386,048     1.0 s           7.42%              249 MB

LatticeLibrary ships `RegularCellArray` and `ConformalCellArray` and NEITHER
grades the cell size spatially, so the transition BETWEEN two cell sizes could
not be measured without writing a new `ICellArray` — see R5 item 7.


S3.c — WHAT IT HOLDS, WHAT IT WRITES, AND WHAT THE EXPORT WEIGHS
------------------------------------------------------------------
★ THE FIRST NUMBER TO GET RIGHT IS THE VOXEL PITCH, and getting it wrong makes
the whole comparison meaningless. His DESIGN grid is 1.705 mm; his struts are
0.45 to 2.4 mm. A lattice rendered at the design pitch cannot resolve its own
struts and produces a triangle count that means nothing. The rows below are at
LATTICE pitches, rung 068, 4.6026 mm cell, von Mises driver:

  PicoGK    min strut     lattice    mesh          STL          VDB      VDB vs   total   peak
  voxel     in voxels     gen (s)    triangles     (bytes)      (bytes)  STL      wall    RSS
  0.45 mm     1.00          9.8      4,862,876    243,143,884   24,422,029  10.0x  18.0 s  1.11 GB
  0.30 mm     1.50         14.9     11,360,620    568,031,084   71,375,770   8.0x  29.8 s  1.95 GB
  0.20 mm     2.25         24.6     26,276,120  1,313,806,084  191,146,722   6.9x  62.8 s  3.05 GB

  (for reference, at his DESIGN pitch of 1.705 mm — too coarse to be a lattice —
   192,420 triangles, 9.6 MB STL, 761,454-byte VDB, 4.8 s, 267 MB.)

★ THE TRIANGLE COUNT IS A PROPERTY OF THE GEOMETRY, NOT OF THE TOOL. At 0.20 mm
PicoGK produces 26,276,120 triangles for ONE rung. His four variants are
102,972,348 triangles — 25.7 million per variant. They agree. Nobody is going to
make that number smaller by changing kernels.

★ WHAT CHANGES IS THAT PICOGK NEVER HAS TO WRITE THEM. The lattice exists as a
sparse OpenVDB narrow band; the mesh is manufactured only to export. At the same
0.20 mm pitch the VDB is **182.3 MB against 1252.9 MB of STL — 6.9x** — and the
VDB is the LIVE form, the one every boolean, offset and fillet operates on.
Scaled to his four variants, ~5.1 GB of triangles becomes ~0.75 GB of VDB.

★ AND IT IS FAST. The whole graded lattice — read the field, build the cell
array, taper 28,665 cells' beams against 1.8 million thickness lookups, clip to
the design, mesh it and write it — is **18 to 63 seconds** depending on pitch.

MEMORY IS BOUNDED AND MODEST: 1.1 to 3.0 GB peak resident for a 26-million-
triangle lattice, because what is resident is the narrow band and not the
triangles.


S3.d — WHERE THE CERTIFICATE WOULD HAVE TO SIT
------------------------------------------------
PicoGK does no FEA, its maintainers say so, and nothing here attempted to
certify anything with it. Stating it plainly, because the answer is not "add a
solver to PicoGK":

  * The lattice PicoGK produces is a VOXEL FIELD (an OpenVDB level set), not a
    density per design voxel. core's certification path takes a density per
    design voxel: `analyze_fixed_design`
    (core/include/topopt/analyze.hpp:306) is `(grid, params, density, bcs,
    loads, material, ...)`, and `grade_lattice`
    (core/include/topopt/grading.hpp:442) is `(grid, density, demand, region,
    params, iso)`.

  * So a PicoGK lattice can only be certified TODAY by throwing away what makes
    it worth having: down-sampling its narrow band back onto the 128 x 31 x 118
    design lattice and reading a per-voxel occupancy off it. That round trip
    discards precisely the sub-voxel strut placement the implicit kernel exists
    to provide, and it is the same trip his own pipeline already makes.

  * The honest place for the certificate is where it already is: on the DESIGN,
    before the lattice is materialised, with the lattice's homogenised
    properties standing in for it — which is what core's composite octet tensor
    path does. PicoGK changes how the lattice is REPRESENTED and EXPORTED. It
    does not change what is certified, and it does not remove the need for the
    homogenisation.
