No bridging geometry was emitted and none was needed: the levels are dyadic and

ONE ISOLATED COMPONENT, AND IT IS NOT AT THE SEAM. 16 triangles (one strut), bbox
[-6.0, 1.0, 3.0] to [-4.5, 2.5, 6.0]. The seam plane is z = 12.0 and both declared
regions live at z >= 8, so this fragment is ~6 mm below the nearer of them — outside
every declared include region. That is the CELL-OVERHANG behaviour PR #298 diagnosed
and did not fix (the certification mask is include-scoped per VOXEL while cell
activation is whole-cell from any masked voxel, so an activated cell reaches past the
region), showing up here as one clipped strut with nothing to weld to.

SO: bar R5's "isolated fragments must be zero" is NOT met — the count is 1, not 0 —
and the cause is named and located rather than left silent. It is not a seam defect
and it is not new to fit; a finer cell simply makes the overhang produce a smaller,
lonelier piece of geometry. Reported, not fixed: fixing it means making cell
activation region-aware, which moves the emitted mask on every existing graded path.

THE COMPONENT COUNT (8,928) IS NOT A FRAGMENT COUNT. The generator emits each strut
as its own capsule mesh and does not weld them, so two struts that interpenetrate are
still two components by shared-vertex connectivity. That is why the isolated test is
proximity-based at the job's own line width, and why the count of components is not
by itself evidence of anything.
