THE RULE: "the last accepted rung" — ResultsModel.buildTabs, `recommendedIndex =
variants.count - 1`. Rungs arrive heaviest-solid-first, so the last one is the
LIGHTEST SOLID. On a run with no lattice that is exactly right and nothing here
changes it.

THE INVERSION: the solid masses DESCEND down the ladder (543.73 → 360.30) and the
latticed masses ASCEND (215.16 → 246.38). They are monotone in opposite
directions, so the rung the recommendation picks is the one whose latticed object
is the HEAVIEST of the four.

  recommended rung 0.26  → its lattice weighs 246.38 g
  lightest lattice 0.68  → 215.16 g
  penalty for following the recommendation: 31.22 g, 12.7 %

WHY, mechanically: the lattice replaces printed material with 26.5–90 %-density
octet. A heavier rung has MORE material inside the include region to replace, so
it gains more from the substitution; a rung that has already had its material
optimized away has less left to convert and ends up closer to its own solid mass.
The ratio column shows it directly — 0.396 of the solid at rung 0.68, 0.684 at
rung 0.26.

  Fixed cost sanity check: the four latticed objects span only 31 g (215–246)
  while their solids span 183 g (360–544). Most of a latticed object's mass is
  the part of the design the lattice does NOT touch — the solid companion body
  and the shell — which barely moves along the ladder.

AND THE SECOND-ORDER PROBLEM, which is worse for the user than the first: the
lightest latticed object is the one on the 1.95 GB mesh, and the heaviest is the
one on the 740 MB mesh. The better object is also the harder one to bring to the
device. Ranking by latticed mass would systematically point at the least
transferable rung.

WHAT IS **NOT** DECIDED HERE:
  * whether the recommendation should rank latticed objects at all;
  * whether it should rank the pair (rung, lattice) jointly, and by what;
  * whether "Minimize plastic" should mean minimum PRINTED mass (the lattice) or
    minimum optimized volume (the solid) once both exist.
All three change what verdict a user acts on, and the task says not to move that
rule without the maintainer's ruling. `testTheRecommendationPointsAtTheHeaviest
LatticedObject` PINS the current behaviour so that a later change has to be made
deliberately and out loud.

A CAUTION AGAINST GENERALISING THIS. On a different part the ordering reverses.
A four-rung fixture from /private/tmp/m4/C1_base (uniform 6 mm octet, strut
radius 1 mm, no grading) gives lattice masses that RISE with the rung, the same
direction as the solid:

  rung 0.26  11.00 g      rung 0.38  12.57 g
  rung 0.52  14.40 g      rung 0.68  16.49 g

So the recommendation is not reliably backwards — it is UNGROUNDED once a lattice
exists. On his part it points at the heaviest of the four; on a uniform-lattice
part it happens to point at the lightest. Neither is because the rule considered
the question.
