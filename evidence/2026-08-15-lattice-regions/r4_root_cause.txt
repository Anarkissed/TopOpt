=== WHY EVERY SECTOR MEASURED 3.4106 mm — AND IT IS NOT THE STATISTIC ===

Two fixes were tried and BOTH failed identically: the MINIMUM of the
inscribed-sphere thickness, then its MEDIAN. Both returned 3.4106 mm (exactly
2 x the 1.70528 mm spacing) for sectors declared 3.0 / 4.5 / 6.0 / 7.5 mm.

Rather than guess a third statistic, region_extent_probe printed the mask's own
bounding box and the FULL tau distribution per sector:

 sector  declared  layers  mask bbox (i x j x k)   tau: min p25 med p75 p90 max
   0       3.0       2      100 x 2 x 53  (308)    3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411
   1       4.5       3      102 x 1 x 55  (372)    3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411
   2       6.0       4      104 x 1 x 57  (522)    3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411
   3       7.5       4      104 x 2 x 57  (781)    3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411 3.411

★ min == p25 == median == max. The distribution is a POINT. No statistic over it
  could ever have separated the sectors, so neither fix was ever going to work.

★ THE BOUNDING BOXES SAY WHY. The bore's axis is j. Its axial extent is about
  six voxels, and I split it into FOUR axial slices — so each sector is 1-2
  VOXELS TALL along j, while spanning ~100 x ~55 voxels around and through the
  wall. The set's thinnest dimension is therefore the SLICE HEIGHT, ~2 voxels,
  in every sector. The declared depth grows i and k (100 -> 104, 53 -> 57) and
  never touches the binding dimension.

★★ SO region_thinnest_extent_mm WAS CORRECT ALL ALONG. It reports the region's
   thinnest dimension, and for these sectors that dimension really is the slice
   height. It is also CONSISTENT with the analytic law it mirrors: a "face"
   lattice region takes min(depth, 2*half_u, 2*half_w), which on a 2-voxel-tall
   slab would likewise return the slab height, not the depth.

★★ THE DEFECT WAS THE EXPERIMENT. I split a six-voxel-tall bore four ways, which
   makes the split height binding and makes the depth incapable of mattering —
   then read the resulting flat table as a defect in the code, twice, and
   "fixed" it twice.

WHAT A VALID DEMONSTRATION NEEDS: sectors whose OTHER dimensions are large
compared with the declared depth, so the depth is the thinnest dimension and
therefore the one the fit law reads. Re-run on face 15 (the part's largest,
1.71e4 mm^2, 6081 voxels one layer deep) split 1x2 — see r4_sectors_large.txt.
