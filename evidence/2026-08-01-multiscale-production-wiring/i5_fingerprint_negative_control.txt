I5 — THE RECYCLE/DEFLATION FINGERPRINT NEGATIVE CONTROL
========================================================
Bar: a fixture where two designs share the SAME scalar-modulus-equivalent field
but DIFFERENT cubic tensors must INVALIDATE the fingerprint. The test must FAIL
against the unfixed fingerprint (i.e. the failure mode — silent reuse of a
stale basis/coarse operator — must actually be caught, not assumed).

Where the modulus-keyed fingerprint lives (audit result):
  * RecycleSession (recycle.cpp) carries NO modulus fingerprint at all: its
    carried basis keys on (n, configured k) only, and at the production rebuild
    cycle = 1 EVERY solve re-forms E = U^T A U with k exact matvecs of the
    CURRENT operator inside RecycleSession::setup — E is never stale, and the
    additive form U E^-1 U^T is SPD for ANY carried U, so exactness is
    unconditional (recycle.hpp's documented contract; exercised on the cubic
    operator by test_matfree_cubic section 7).
  * geneo.cpp's moduli_fingerprint IS the modulus-keyed fingerprint of the
    deflation family: geneo_solve_begin compares it to decide REUSE (action 1,
    coarse operator untouched) vs REFRESH (action 2, mandatory rebuild of
    V^T A V — phase 2 §P6 measured divergence from stale reuse). Before this
    task it hashed ONLY ctx.elem_youngs (+ poisson): blind to c11/c12/c44 and
    the lattice mask.

The fix: moduli_fingerprint now hashes the lattice presence tag, mask, c11,
c12, c44 (geneo.cpp). MfSolveContext carries the lattice arrays.

Test: test_matfree_cubic.cpp section 6 — cubic_checkerboard(33, 4, c44_scale):
design A (c44_scale=1.0) and design B (c44_scale=1.5) have memcmp-IDENTICAL
scalar youngs fields and DIFFERENT c44 arrays.
