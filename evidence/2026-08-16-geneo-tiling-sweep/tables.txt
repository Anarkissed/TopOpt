§1(a)  WHAT EACH TILING MEANS IN VOXELS ON HIS 128 x 31 x 118 GRID
==============================================================================
PER-AXIS, NEVER A MINIMUM. `tile_cores` (core/src/fea/geneo.cpp:167)
steps x, y and z independently, so the subdomain count is
ceil(nx/c)*ceil(ny/c)*ceil(nz/c) and NOT (n/c)^3 for any single n.
His part is a 4.1:1 slab (128 long, 31 thin); a tiling keyed to the
thin axis would size every axis to 31 and multiply the subdomain count
— and N_t, and the refresh the gate is priced against — with it.
Pinned by test_geneo's R6 block against a 24:1 synthetic slab.

32 is shown but is NOT a sweep point: with y at a single tile it
confounds 'coarser tiling' with 'no tiling on one axis'. 24 is the
last point that still tiles every axis, which is why the sweep stops
there.

==============================================================================
§1(b)  N_t PER TILING — the one-solve triage
==============================================================================
N_t is decided on the FIRST solve: the basis is built once per
structure and its dimension is a property of that build. So one
design iteration per rung answers this, and does it deterministically
— N_t is a COUNT and a contended host cannot change it.

★ TWO N_t COLUMNS, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THEM IS A FINDING.
`N_t first` is the dimension of the basis built on the run's FIRST
stagnating solve. `N_t final` is what the run was still holding at the
end. They differ when the basis DEGRADED and the scheduled rebuild
returned a different dimension — which is what happened at core=12
(718 -> 1123) and did not happen at core=8 (built once, never rebuilt).
Production experiences the FINAL number for most of a run, so the
headline fall is 1.5x and not the 2.35x the first build suggests.

 core  subdomains  N_t first  N_t final  modes/sub  basis MB  refresh 2*N_t  implied thresh
    8         960       1686       1686       1.76     40.31           3372            4726
   12         330        718       1123       3.40     52.86           2246            3600
   16         128        369        603       4.71     53.20           1206            2560
   24          60        208        370       6.17     63.23            740            2094

Against the shipped 8^3 (N_t = 1686):
  core=8   N_t  1686  =  1.00x the shipped   subdomains  1.00x
  core=12  N_t  1123  =  0.67x the shipped   subdomains  0.34x
  core=16  N_t   603  =  0.36x the shipped   subdomains  0.13x
  core=24  N_t   370  =  0.22x the shipped   subdomains  0.06x

If N_t fell exactly with the subdomain count the two ratios would
match. Where N_t falls SLOWER, each larger subdomain is carrying more
modes — block_m = 20 caps it at 20 per subdomain, and that cap is the
mechanism that would flatten the curve.

THE BAR THE THRESHOLD MUST CLEAR: his 160 stagnating solves cost a
MEDIAN of 2,717 plain CG iterations (min 927, max 5,091), against his
observed threshold of 4702. ★ NOT ~4,400 — that is the median of
the 8 declines in his DECISION LOG, which records transitions only and
so holds the hardest 5 % of the 156 declines rather than a sample.

A tiling whose implied threshold drops under ~2,700 is one where the
gate starts engaging routinely. ★ THAT IS A NECESSARY CONDITION AND NOT
A SUFFICIENT ONE, and on this part it is not even a desirable one on its
own: cost_model.py shows the gate ENGAGING MORE while the run gets
SLOWER, because engagements near the threshold lose. Read cost_model.txt
and the §2(b) trade table before drawing anything from this column.

==============================================================================
§2(b)  DEFLATION QUALITY PER TILING — does a smaller basis deflate WORSE?
==============================================================================
★ THIS IS THE AXIS THAT SEPARATES §2(a) FROM §2(b), and it is readable
from the triage without any extra run. On every point, rung 0's first
solve is the BUILD solve: it burns exactly kGeneoTriggerIters = 500
plain iterations, then deflates to convergence. So

      deflated tail  =  cg_iters(rung 0, iter 1)  -  500

is the number of DEFLATED iterations that basis needed to finish a solve
plain Jacobi could not. A coarser tiling buys a cheaper refresh (2*N_t)
and pays for it here. If the tail grows faster than N_t shrinks, the
lever is exhausted and the answer is §2(b).

 core     N_t  rung0 cg   burn   tail  refresh 2*N_t  engage thresh  all-in equiv
   12     718       935    500    435           1436           2806          5112
   16     369       806    500    306            738           1850          3200
   24     208       985    500    485            416           1886          3272
    8    1686       972    500    472           3372           4816          9132

READ IT AGAINST THE PLAIN ALTERNATIVE — AND AGAINST THE RIGHT ONE.
★ A CORRECTION THIS TABLE CARRIES SO NOBODY REPEATS IT. His TYPICAL
stagnating solve costs 2,717 CG (median of all 160 rows of his
iterations.csv). It does NOT cost ~4,400: that figure is the median of
the 8 declines in his DECISION LOG, and the log records transitions
only, so those 8 are the solves that immediately followed an engage —
systematically the hardest, 5 % of the 156 declines. Using 4,400 puts
the break-even at N_t = 669 when it is really near 256, i.e. it calls a
tiling a win that is not one.

★ THE BREAK-EVEN, IN CLOSED FORM. Substituting the gate's own cost
model (geneo.hpp: refresh = 2*N_t, a deflated iteration = 2 plain):

    all-in equiv = threshold + 2*N_t + 2*tail
                 = (2*N_t + 500 + 2*tail) + 2*N_t + 2*tail
                 = 4*N_t + 4*tail + 500

so beating a MEDIAN 2,717-iteration plain solve needs

    ★  N_t + tail  <  554  ★

At the shipped 8^3 that sum is 1686 + 472 = 2158, i.e. 3.9x over.

★ AND THIS CLOSED FORM IS THE COARSE VIEW, NOT THE ANSWER. It compares
against ONE representative solve. `cost_model.py` does the honest
version — it runs the gate's rule against ALL 160 of his measured solve
costs, one at a time, and finds a NON-MONOTONE result this one-number
form cannot show: shrinking N_t from 1686 to 718 makes the run WORSE,
because it drops the threshold into the middle of his cost distribution
and the gate starts engaging on solves that were about to finish plain.
Read cost_model.txt before quoting anything from this block.

AND ONE FRAMING THIS TABLE DELIBERATELY DOES NOT USE. If a hot basis
could deflate from iteration 0 — no burn — the cost would be just
2*N_t + 2*N_defl, which at 8^3 is 3372 + 944 = 4316 against 5,412 for
his hardest plain solve: already a win. The SHIPPED ski-rental gate
forbids that, by design and for a measured reason (arming every solve
lost 1.25x on wall — 2026-08-02-geneo-standing-probe). So the gate
policy is itself a term in the economics, and a tiling that made N_t
small enough would reopen the standing-preconditioner question that was
closed at N_t ~ 1,686. That is a SEPARATE proposal and is not armed
here; it is flagged because the sweep is what would justify re-asking.

==============================================================================
§1(c)  TOTAL CG ITERATIONS PER TILING — ★ THE FIGURE OF MERIT
==============================================================================
★ TOTAL CG ITERATIONS, NOT THE ARMED COUNT. A smaller basis that
engages on every solve and deflates badly is WORSE than a large one
that engages rarely. The armed/declined columns are a DIAGNOSTIC of
what the gate did; the total-CG column is the result. Read them in
that order.

R1: iteration counts are the EVIDENCE (deterministic, contention-immune);
wall is CONTEXT and on this host it was measured under load — see
host_load.txt. Every wall figure here is from a Release build
(CMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release, recorded in build_type.txt).

★ ONE CONFOUND IN THIS COLUMN, NAMED RATHER THAN BURIED, AND IT IS
LARGER THAN IT LOOKS. GenEO is exact to the SOLVER TOLERANCE, not to
the last bit, so two tilings finish rung 0 with fields agreeing to
~1e-8 and hand slightly different designs to the next rung.

How small that design difference is, and how big its effect: at core=8
and core=12 the certified margins agree at EVERY rung to all six
printed digits (1751.28 / 1383.13 / 514.952) — yet rung 1 cost 1,121 CG
at core=8 and 1,372 at core=12, a 22 % swing. A perturbation too small
to move the margin in the 6th digit moves the iteration count by a
fifth, because CG counts are sensitive to where a residual lands
relative to a fixed tolerance.

CONSEQUENCE: at --iters 1 a total-CG difference below roughly 20 %
between tilings CANNOT be attributed to the lever. This is why the
handoff leans on the cost-model decomposition (N_t, tail, threshold —
all trajectory-independent, all read per solve) and treats total CG as
a confirming measurement rather than the sole one.

R5  THE CERTIFIED MARGIN AT EVERY TILING — GenEO IS EXACT
==============================================================================
Every term in the preconditioner is SPD, so the deflation changes the
CG ROUTE and never the converged field or the stopping test. A margin
that moved with the tiling would mean that claim is false, so this is
a real check and not a formality.

     vf          core=12          core=16          core=24           core=8
  0.680    1751.27987(1)    1751.27986(1)    1751.27987(1)    1751.27987(1)
  0.520     1383.1277(1)     1383.1277(1)     1383.1277(1)     1383.1277(1)
  0.380    514.951762(1)    514.951762(1)    514.951762(1)    514.951762(1)
  0.260    106.908685(1)    106.908685(1)    106.908685(1)    106.908685(1)

(value(accepted)) — accepted=1 is an ACCEPT verdict.

★ THE RIGHT BAR IS NOT BIT-IDENTITY, AND SAYING SO MATTERS.
GenEO is exact to the SOLVER TOLERANCE (1e-8), not to the last bit: it
changes the CG route, so two tilings reach the same field to 1e-8 and
then hand slightly different fields to an optimiser that can amplify
the difference over a ladder. So a margin that moves by ~1e-6 relative
is the expected behaviour of an exact method, and a margin that moves
by PERCENT is the thing that would falsify the exactness claim. Both
are reported below rather than collapsed into one pass/fail.
  core=16 vs core=12: max rel move 5.710e-09 at vf=0.68; verdict flips: 0 — consistent with exactness
  core=24 vs core=12: IDENTICAL; verdict flips: 0 — consistent with exactness
  core=8 vs core=12: IDENTICAL; verdict flips: 0 — consistent with exactness
