★ ORGANIC SPENDS MATERIALLY MORE MARGIN — 36.7 % against 29.9 %, 6.8 points worse.
Per §4(c) that is a finding about the method and it is in §0 of the handoff.

★ AND THE CONFOUND IS STATED, BECAUSE WITHOUT IT THE NUMBER IS MISLEADING: organic put
in 20.6 % LESS MATERIAL (797.56 vs 1004.46 mm^3). Less material is a softer load path,
which raises stress in the SOLID region, and `lattice_margin_worst_case` is the SOLID
region's margin. So part of that 6.8 points is simply less mass, not worse mass.

★ THE NUMBER THAT POINTS THE OTHER WAY, AND IT IS THE DAYNES EFFECT: organic's own
STRUTS carry 2.8x LESS STRESS — 0.00145738 against 0.00406431 MPa — while carrying
20.6 % less material. Aligning struts with the principal directions is exactly the
claim that each strut then works less hard for the same job, and that is what this
measures.

★ WHAT IT DOES NOT SETTLE, AND MUST NOT BE READ AS SETTLING: this is NOT a
matched-density comparison, so it does not establish that organic is more or less
efficient than the octet cell. §4(d) asks whether organic is worth its complexity
structurally; answering that needs a MATCHED-MASS run, which nobody has done. What is
established: at 20 % less material organic's struts are 2.8x less stressed, and the
composite's solid-region margin is 6.8 points worse.

★ AND ONE STRUCTURAL FACT WORTH KEEPING: DOUBLED AND STEPPED CERTIFY IDENTICALLY, to
the last digit, and that is BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by coincidence. The homogenised
tensor is a function of RELATIVE DENSITY ALONE and both take their density from the same
law; only the CELL differs, and the cell does not enter the tensor. Their difference is
purely geometric — soup volume 2168.76 vs 3084.84 mm^3 for the same certified object.
