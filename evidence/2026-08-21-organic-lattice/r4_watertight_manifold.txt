--- R4, READ HONESTLY ---
CLOSED: boundary_edges = 0 on BOTH. Every primitive is a closed solid and there is no
hole anywhere in either mesh. That is the half of R4 that a downstream slicer needs.

OUTWARD-WOUND, and this is the positive control: the mesh's SIGNED VOLUME (divergence
theorem over the welded triangles) equals the generator's own ANALYTIC per-primitive sum
to the last digit printed — organic 1403.7347 vs 1403.7, octet 6680.5787 vs 6680.6. Two
independent computations of the same quantity agreeing is what says the winding is
outward and nothing was emitted inside-out. (A mixed soup is exactly the defect task
2026-08-09-fix-inward-wound-normals found and lattice_gen.cpp's icosahedron table warns
about; this is that test, run against organic.)

NOT EDGE-2-MANIFOLD, AND NEITHER IS THE SHIPPED PATH. Both meshes report non-manifold
edges after welding, because a strut lattice is an INTERPENETRATING UNION OF CLOSED
SOLIDS — lattice_gen.hpp says so in its first paragraph, and PR 201 closed a real print
on exactly that union. Coincident vertices between two primitives weld into an edge used
by more than two triangles. The octet reference carries 164,456 of them on this fixture;
organic carries 127,080. So "non-manifold" here is a property of the SOUP CONTRACT this
codebase already ships and prints, not a defect introduced by tracing.

★ THE ONE THING THAT WAS A DEFECT, AND IT WAS FOUND BY THIS CHECK: the first version
emitted a node ball at BOTH ends of every span, so every interior polyline vertex carried
two identical icosahedra — 11,364 balls for 5,682 spans. Deduplicating them removed
81,900 triangles (20 % of the mesh) and 81,990 non-manifold edges that existed for
nothing. The measurement above is after that fix.
