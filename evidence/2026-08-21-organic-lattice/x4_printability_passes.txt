★ ORGANIC'S PRINTABILITY PASSES — WHAT WAS WRONG, WHAT FIXED IT, MEASURED ON THE
  SHIPPED MESH AND NOT ON THE GENERATOR'S OWN BOOKKEEPING
  (task 2026-08-21-organic-lattice; every defect below was found by the MAINTAINER
   looking at an exported STL, after a measurement of mine had said it was fine)

── 0. THE MEASUREMENT, AND ITS CONTROLS ────────────────────────────────────────
deadend_probe.cpp reads a WELDED STL and nothing else. Two stages:
  1. candidates — a surface vertex whose neighbourhood inside R=1.6 mm is one-sided
     along the local principal axis. A FILTER, deliberately loose.
  2. verdict    — how many members cross out of a ball of radius Rb=2.0 mm about it.
     1 limb = DEAD END. 2 = mid-member. 3+ = junction.

★ STAGE 1 ALONE IS NOT A VERDICT. It reports 269 on the coupon the maintainer PRINTED
  SUCCESSFULLY, and 7 of the 8 deepest "interior" flags have 2-3 limbs. Only stage 2
  is reportable. An earlier detector of mine returned ZERO on both a defective and a
  fixed file — a broken instrument, not a pass.

CONTROLS (Rb = 2.0 mm):
  coupon the maintainer PRINTED      97k verts    31 dead ends   32.0 per 100k
  octet / doubled, what we ship     1093k verts   50 dead ends    4.6 per 100k

★ THE NUMBER IS SENSITIVE TO Rb and is only meaningful against those controls at a
  FIXED Rb. Measured sweep, clean / skin:
     Rb=1.2  188 / 305     Rb=1.6   26 / 144     Rb=2.0    6 / 31
     Rb=2.6    1 /   5     Rb=3.2    0 /   3
  The RATIO holds at ~5x throughout, so skin's excess is real; the absolute figure is
  not a physical constant.

── 1. THE STRUCTURAL BLOCKER (this is the generalisable part) ──────────────────
generate_organic_lattice wrote each strut to the sink THE MOMENT IT WAS CLIPPED. Every
later pass could therefore only APPEND — ties, ground legs, anchor balls. A whisker,
once written, was written, and every fix for weeks was additive.
FIX: record the whole span list, run the passes, EMIT ONCE at the end.
Nothing that CUTS is expressible until that is done.

── 2. SUPPORT != CONTAINMENT (the defect six rounds of fixes never touched) ─────
The free-end test asked "is this tip inside some other span's solid". A BUNDLE of
near-parallel struts that all END at the same point satisfies that about each other.
Attribution found it: tagging every span with the pass that emitted it, dumping the
final list, and correlating against the dead ends the SHIPPED mesh shows —
★ EVERY ONE of the 19 survivors had ~6 SPAN TIPS CONVERGING ON IT.

The rule that works — support is about what the neighbour DOES at the contact:
  INTERIOR contact  the tip lands on another span's BODY (a T on a beam).  supported
  TIP contact       the other span ends here too. Supported only if it LEADS AWAY
                    (dot(away, out) > cos 105 deg). A sibling folding back is NOT.
  unsupported tips found  221 -> 663        pruned  9 spans -> ~1,600 over 18 rounds

── 3. NODE MERGE — the other half of the treatment, and it is in the source paper ─
Daynes et al., Materials & Design 127:215-223: at the core boundary small cells appear
and "nodes are either MERGED or DELETED". Only deleting was implemented.
  merged 8,743 shared nodes from 25,144 endpoints; 1,058 spans collapsed
  dead ends 54 -> 6

── 4. STRANDED PIECES ARE DELETED, NOT PROPPED UP ──────────────────────────────
The ground-tie repair tied an unreachable crumb down with a vertical leg — on the cube
that was a 0.42 mm pole standing ~30 mm tall at a corner with a crumb on top,
photographed by the maintainer. A piece below kOrganicStrandedKeepFraction (2 %) of
total length is now DELETED. Ground-tie legs on the cube: 0.

── 5. A FINISH IS A LOOK, NOT A REPAIR (the maintainer's ruling) ───────────────
The finish ran BEFORE the prune and changed what got cut — 1,588 spans for `clean`
against 1,210 for `skin`. That made a finish a structural intervention wearing an
aesthetic name. The finish now runs LAST; the only thing it may remove is its own
joins. PROVEN BY HASH, not asserted:

  core sha256   8f3f7506e616aa8c (identical)
  finish joins             0        218       2921

  dead ends (Rb 2.0)       6          9         41
  per 100k verts         1.6        2.4        9.5
  watertight/manifold    yes        yes        yes
  surface components       1          1          1
  off the plate            0          0          0

★ TWO FURTHER TRAPS, BOTH MEASURED:
  (a) A finish keyed on LEFTOVERS is not a look. Selecting "clipped ends" made the
      finish's size depend on how much the prune had removed — 661 candidates before
      the reorder, 121 after, same part, same look asked for. It now selects the
      SURFACE NODES OF THE FINISHED LATTICE.
  (b) A finish that REDRAWS the lattice is invisible. Joining each surface node to its
      nearest neighbour joined it to the other end of a strut it was already attached
      to: 1,889 of 2,615 joins duplicated an existing member endpoint-for-endpoint,
      83 % of a sample lay wholly inside existing material, mean join 1.23 mm against
      a 3.10 mm separation — and all three finishes rendered as the SAME PICTURE
      through four rounds of the maintainer's renders. Filters: not already joined,
      >= 0.55 of the local separation, both ends facing within 50 deg, not buried.
      Result: 0 duplicates, mean join 2.34 mm, and the difference is visible.

── 6. AN HONEST NEGATIVE RESULT ────────────────────────────────────────────────
38.6 % of net nodes (839 of 2,173) carried exactly ONE join — a net thread that just
stops. Hypothesis: that is what the surface dead-end count reads. Closing the net
(the fallback now runs while a node is short of TWO) cut degree-one nodes to 68
(3.1 %), and rim's from 150 to 30.
★ IT MADE THE DEAD-END COUNT WORSE: skin 31 -> 41, because closing the net added 717
  joins and ~2,000 mm of material. THE HYPOTHESIS IS REFUTED. The change is kept
  because a net with 839 dangling threads is not a net, and the cost is recorded here
  rather than argued away. 18 of the 41 carry a net tip, 23 are core nodes; 37 of 41
  are within 1 mm of a face.
