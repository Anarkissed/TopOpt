★★ THE BUG THE INVARIANT CAUGHT, AND IT WAS MINE.

The first three attempts at the organic export were REFUSED outright:
  190 of 34,776 vertices outside the shell, worst 0.1802 mm  (unattributed)
   70 of 32,916 vertices outside the shell, worst 0.0622 mm  (interior strut pass)

Two separate defects, and the second is the interesting one.

1. NODE BALLS WERE NOT GUARDED. The octet generator drops any node ball whose SOLID
   would breach the eroded region (lattice_gen.cpp's interior-node loop): the clip
   certificate covers the swept STRUT, not a sphere about its cut end. Organic emitted
   them unconditionally. Same guard added, drops counted in `dropped_nodes`.

2. ★ I READ `LatticeClipSpan::t0/t1` AS FRACTIONS. They are ARC-LENGTH IN MILLIMETRES
   — lattice_boundary.hpp says so in the struct's own comment, "0 at a, |b-a| at b".
   So `a + (b-a)*t1` placed a whole span's far end |b-a| TIMES too far along, and the
   strut ran straight out through the shell. Scaling by the UNIT direction fixes it.

   ★ WHAT ACTUALLY FOUND IT was not reading the code — I had reasoned twice, wrongly,
   that the certified Lipschitz clip made this impossible. It was the ATTRIBUTION:
   feeding organic the same LatticeGenObserver the octet path uses turned
   "emitted by the unattributed pass" into "emitted by the interior strut pass",
   which ruled out the node balls and left exactly one candidate. The observer is now
   wired permanently for that reason.

All three now write a file with ZERO protruding vertices.
