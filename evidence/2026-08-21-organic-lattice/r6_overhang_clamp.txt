★ R6 — THE OVERHANG CLAMP'S EFFECT, AT THE DEFAULT AND AT 45. READ THIS WITH §0(i).

THE DEFAULT IS DISARMED, AND THAT IS A MEASURED CHOICE, NOT AN OMISSION.
The maintainer printed a traced coupon with a 41.78 mm LONGEST UNSUPPORTED RUN, supports
off, and it came out clean (evidence/2026-08-20-lattice-only-grading/r4b_PRINT_RESULT.md).
That is a direct experimental refutation of the 45-degree rule as a hard blocker on this
machine. Re-imposing it by default would discard the stress alignment that is the entire
justification for tracing, in the name of a constraint this printer demonstrably does not
have. §2(b) asks for a parameter with a REPORTED default, and this is it: 0 disarms, the
angle is `grading.organic_overhang_angle_deg`, and the counterfactual is reported on
every run whether the clamp is armed or not.

WHAT THE COUNTERFACTUAL SAYS. With the clamp OFF, 64.67 % of traced curve segments sit
outside a 45-degree cone about the build direction. That is the fraction a textbook
overhang gate would have re-angled — nearly two thirds of the lattice. It is large
because it is the correct answer: the principal stress directions on this part are
mostly NOT aligned with +Z, and following them is the point.

WHAT THE CLAMP DOES WHEN ARMED. 74.15 % of RK4 steps are projected onto the cone and
81.62 % of curves are touched — and afterwards EXACTLY 0.000000 of the traced curve
segments lie outside 45 degrees. The clamp is exact, not approximate, because it runs
IN THE TRACING LOOP (§2a): each step's direction is projected onto the nearest in-cone
direction before the step is taken, so the emitted segment is in-cone by construction.
There is no repair pass and there is nothing left over to repair.

THE RESIDUAL IS CONNECTORS, AND IT IS STRUCTURAL. With the clamp armed, 20.81 % of ALL
segments are still outside the cone, and every one of them is a CONNECTOR (82.16 % of
connectors). Connectors are never clamped, deliberately: a connector is the shortest
join between two curves, and re-angling it breaks the join — which is the one thing R3
exists to protect. A connector is also at most one separation long (about 3 mm here),
so it is a short bridge, not a long unsupported run. The 41.78 mm coupon is evidence
about exactly that: long unsupported runs printed clean.

THE CLAMP IS ALSO NOT FREE, AND THE TABLE SHOWS THE PRICE. Armed, the layout changes:
3,167 curves instead of 3,056 and a slightly tighter median separation (3.020 vs 3.069
mm), because a clamped curve turns toward the build axis and therefore leaves the region
sooner and gets re-seeded. Connectivity is unharmed (31 curves under two connections
against 34; 3 components either way).
