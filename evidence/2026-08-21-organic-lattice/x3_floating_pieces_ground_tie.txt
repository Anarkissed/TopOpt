★ "I CAN'T PRINT IT — A FEW PIECES ARE JUST FLOATING." He was right again, and my
connectivity bar could not see it.

★★ CONNECTEDNESS IS NOT PRINTABILITY, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE LESSON.
R3 measured whether the emitted solids TOUCH EACH OTHER: 99.99 % of length in one
component, 2.0 mm stranded. All true, and all silent about whether the material can be
BUILT. A printer lays material bottom-up. A strut welded to the lattice at its top and
starting in mid-air at its bottom is connected AND unprintable.

MEASURED ON THE FILE HE TRIED TO PRINT:
  soup   14,969 surface components, 14,776 never reaching the plate
  welded      1 surface component, reaching the plate
★ So the SOUP is the wrong file to print, and that alone explains most of it: a slicer
that analyses the mesh calls every shell that misses the plate a floating body.

★ BUT THE WELDED FILE WAS NOT INNOCENT EITHER. Rasterising the emitted solids and
flooding from the lowest occupied layer found 52 FLOATING VOXELS in 9 pieces —
"a few pieces", exactly as he described. `keep_largest_component` on the weld hides them
by DELETING them, which is worse than fixing them: material the tracer placed silently
vanishes from the file.

THE FIX IS HIS OWN COUPON'S, APPLIED TO THE POST-CLIP SPANS
(evidence/2026-08-20-lattice-only-grading/coupon/graded_coupon.cpp): rasterise, flood
from the lowest occupied layer, and drop a VERTICAL LEG from each unreached component's
own lowest voxel to the ground layer; re-flood; repeat up to 12 rounds.

MEASURED, on the 40 mm cube:
  floating voxels BEFORE repair : 52
  ground-tie legs added         : 9   (one round was enough)
  ★ floating voxels AFTER       : 0
  welded body                   : 1 component, reaches the plate,
                                  0 boundary edges, 0 non-manifold edges,
                                  volume 3721.249 mm^3 (was 3713.626 — the legs)

★ WHY IT FLOODS FROM THE LATTICE'S OWN BASE AND NOT FROM THE PART. The spans this sees
are the lattice only; the solid shell and the companion body are ground too, and are not
in the list. Flooding from the lattice's own lowest layer is therefore CONSERVATIVE — in
a real part it may add a leg the shell would have made unnecessary, and it can never
omit one that is needed.

★ AND IT REPORTS RATHER THAN REFUSES, DELIBERATELY, WHICH IS A DEPARTURE FROM THE
COUPON. The coupon refuses when anything still floats, and it can: it builds its own
base slab, so its ground is complete. Mine is not — a piece genuinely tied to the shell
would look floating to this flood. Refusing on an incomplete definition of ground would
reject correct jobs. So `floating_voxels_after_repair` is on every organic receipt, and
a non-zero value is the number to act on.

WHAT IS STILL TRUE OF THE SOUP: it remains thousands of interpenetrating closed shells,
by the same contract the shipped octet export has always used. The welded file is the
one to print. That is now stated at the top of the handoff rather than in a caption.
