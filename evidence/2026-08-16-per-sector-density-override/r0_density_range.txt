★ READING CHOSEN: KEEP THE DERIVED CELL, move only the density.
  * the strut becomes cell*phi(rho) and RISES with rho, so the region stays
    printable and stays at exactly N* cells/member — it KEEPS CERTIFYING;
  * a rho below the derived one implies a sub-bead strut and REFUSES with
    the number, because at a fixed cell the derived density IS the floor of
    the valid range.

★ WHAT THE OTHER READING WOULD HAVE DONE (cell = w/phi(rho), the table above):
  the strut is pinned at exactly 0.4200 mm for EVERY density, so nothing
  could ever refuse — but cells/member falls with rho. On the 6.8211 mm body
  a stated 0.25 gives 3.52 cells/member: the region silently stops
  certifying. Both are honest; the chosen one keeps certification and
  refuses what it cannot do, the other trades certification away without
  being asked.

★ THE VALID RANGE PER REGION (§2d) is therefore [derived_rho, rho_max]:
    body  6.8211 mm  ->  [0.4283, 0.8999]   a 2.1x range
    body 13.6422 mm  ->  [0.1386, 0.8999]   a 6.5x range
  Both are usefully wide, which answers the BLOCKED-STOP asking whether an
  override is expressible at all at his cell size. A THINNER region has a
  narrower range, and that is the honest thing for the app to show before
  he types a number rather than after.
