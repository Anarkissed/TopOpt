# Lattice rebuild — the rules (stage A)

Working draft 4, 2026-11-10, LatticeReview. Companion to `00-proposal.md`.

Each rule below is something the layout must do. Where the rule is the maintainer's, his words and the date are given, with the place in today's code where they are recorded. A ledger of every dated ruling comment in the lattice code (526 entries, verbatim) has been read in full; this draft holds every rule in it that decides geometry for cell lattices. Rulings about organic lattices are listed in section 10 and wait for the organic proposal. Rules about the look of panels and legends are not in scope here.

Status of each rule: **his** (a ruling of his), **agreed** (settled on 2026-11-10 for the rebuild), **proposed** (mine, for him to accept or change).

Section 8 held seven places where two rulings pulled against each other. He settled all seven on 2026-11-10; the answers are written into the rules and kept in section 8.

## 1. Where lattice goes

| # | Rule | Status and source |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Lattice goes wherever it is asked for. Whether a wall is latticed never depends on whether a stress result has arrived. | his, 2026-08-21: "The rule is we lattice *WHEREVER* it is asked of us" (`LatticeSDFMetal.swift:2263`) |
| D2 | A prism under a face turns existing solid into lattice. It never adds material. | his: "the face-prism only makes what's solid into lattice, it cannot make walls thicker" (`LatticeRegionMask.swift:70`) |
| D3 | The lattice is laid out past the prism and then cut by it, so cells at the edge are whole cells cut, not cells left out. | his, 2026-08-27: "extend the lattices out beyond the face-prism, then use the prism to cut off the excess" (`LatticePreviewOccupancy.swift:808`) |
| D4 | Start from All lattice: one layout for the whole part. | his, 2026-11-10 (ruling 2) |
| D5 | **Protect skin** is a solid face, at exactly the depth stated, with the lattice right behind it. At 0 mm it is the thinnest face that prints (two line widths). It is for faces that must stay a real surface: mounting, contact. It is a different thing from the Finish called Skin (S3). The cells start at the skin's inner side and divide what is left of the wall (C15): the skin never cuts through a cell. A skin on the far face ends the lattice the same way. Built for flat regions; under a curved region or the whole part a skin still cuts the cells where it falls. | his, 2026-10-26 ("exact Protect"), 2026-11-10 (ruling 3), and 2026-11-10 16:41: "Protect skin is a solid sheet. The skin I mentioned is the Lattice Finish. Two different things." |
| D6 | Where two prisms overlap, the nearer face owns the space; on the same face plane the deeper one; then the earlier one. No lattice beats Lattice. | reviewer's ruling 4, 2026-10-07, and plan note 22 (`stepped_plan.hpp`) |
| D7 | Nothing is ever built inside a pocket to close it: no wall, plate, cap or beam. The far end of a prism inside material is the part's own solid. | his, 2026-09-23, rule R7 (`LatticeSDFMetal.swift:2712`) |
| D8 | Where a wall cannot hold lattice it goes solid. It is never left as a hole. | his, 2026-08-20: "Go solid" (`LatticeMeasuredRegionWidth.swift:113`) |
| D9 | The edge of the lattice is the part's true surface, measured on the mesh. | agreed, 2026-11-10 |
| D10 | A latticed region has the shape of the face it came from, never the rectangle around it. Laying less than was marked, and saying so, beats laying more in silence. | his, 2026-08-18 and 2026-08-21 (`LatticeFaceOutline.swift:2`, `LatticeRegionEmission.swift:189`) |
| D11 | Expand grows or shrinks a region in the face's plane only, either sign; 0 is exactly the face. It never changes the depth. | his, 2026-08-17: "an expand handle for all the other axis *except* the depth"; "also take a negative value" (`LatticeSlabExpand.swift:2`, `:37`) |
| D12 | Only what is set to Lattice is latticed. Everything else stays solid. | his, 2026-08-18 (`LatticeRegionMask.swift:310`) |
| D13 | Each cell has one owner, decided by where its centre is, never by the order the regions were made. Reordering walls never changes the lattice. A cell that loses a contest steps down one size on its own region's grid. | his, 2026-10-03 ("order-dependent ownership would change the lattice when he reorders walls"); reviewer's ruling 6, 2026-10-07 (`LatticeOctreeBake.swift:962`, `:1028`) |
| D14 | Space inside a region that no cell covers is solid, and is drawn solid, so a slit never looks like a hole the print does not have. The rule is applied by whoever draws or builds: a cut cell is exactly the part of its cube that is lattice of its own region and its own wall, and lattice space in no cell's shape is solid. The layout's own list of such places is complete only to its sample points (strips under about a millimetre can be unlisted). | reviewer, 2026-10-11, item 2 (`LatticeSettings.swift:1026`) |
| D14b | Lattice is consistent up to the surface and across a seam. (1) A cell takes in lattice of ANOTHER region that lies inside its own cube when no cell of that region holds it (the wedge where two regions share a wall and one region's share is too thin for its cells): never held twice. (2) A cube the part's surface cuts so thinly that none of its sample points is lattice is still a cut cell when the rest of the cube is air: the lattice runs out to the surface instead of stopping at a solid crust. A sliver with solid beyond it is not opened (nothing could print over the slot). What stays solid: a wall of one region too thin for that region's own cell (D8). | his, 2026-11-12, on his v3 stand in the slicer: solid bands either side of a latticed strip, and a pocket wall solid on one side of the part and latticed on the other ("These are MAJOR flaws"); "It's either consistently latticed all around the pocket, or it isn't"; "Why can't the pocket just cut the lattices wherever they are positioned" |
| D14c | Every cube of lattice space left solid for want of a cell carries its reason (wall narrower than the smallest cell; too few cells across; a cut piece too small that no neighbour took in; region shallower than the smallest cell; other) and the two sizes, have and need, so the app can say why in one line and offer the settings that open it (his ruling 2026-11-14). | his, 2026-11-14: "there should be a simple explanation of why it doesn't work and one to three ways to fix it" |
| D15 | Protect skin and Lattice on the same face are one slab with one depth. | his, 2026-09-30, ruling 2 (`ProjectModel.swift:836`) |
| D16 | Nothing is printed inside a Keep clear. The part's own solid wins over a Keep clear. | reviewer, 2026-11-03 19:55 (`run_job.cpp:770`) |
| D17 | Material a design box added is solid. The lattice is laid around it, not through it. | reviewer, 2026-11-03 23:05 (`run_job.cpp:7273`) |
| D18 | The same part and settings give the same layout, byte for byte, on every launch. | his, 2026-09-30, ruling 1 (`LatticeFaceOutline.swift:46`) |
| D19 | Start from is the rule for everything a zone, a face-prism or a protection does not cover. Under All lattice a face-prism only changes solid to lattice settings; shrinking it with a negative Expand leaves lattice behind, not solid. Only a No lattice region makes solid inside a lattice. | his, 2026-11-10: "The base setting is always the rule for anything NOT covered by a zone or face-prism or protection." |
| D20 | A face-prism works on a curved face, and on several faces joined into one. Curved: the region follows the face inward by the depth and stops at the face's own outline. Joined: one region, one depth, one outline, no seam between the faces. | his, 2026-11-10: "We definitely need to implement curved face-prisms - as well as unioned ones." |
| D21 | **A zone lattices a space with no face.** A zone is a region placed as a BOX or a CYLINDER in the part's own coordinates instead of picked faces, and is a flat region whose prism is stated instead of read off a face. Box: a centre, three unit axes at right angles and three half sizes; the prism starts on the side at centre − a2 × half2, runs along a2 through the box, is 2 × half2 deep, and its outline is the rectangle ± half0 by ± half1 in (a0, a1), the box's own axes being the cells' in-plane axes. Cylinder: a centre, a unit axis, a radius and a half length; the same prism along the axis, its outline one loop of 96 straight sides whose area is the circle's, its in-plane axes those core gives any face prism of that normal (plane_basis of the axis). Everything a flat face region does then applies unchanged: the role, the part's own material only (a zone reaching outside the part lattices nothing there), Protect skin, rim and cover inside it, Expand (D11, in the plane only), Grade to shape from its outline, its walls, the overlap order (D6: its "face" is the side it starts on), `where` and the cells. Its depth is its own (a region's `depth_mm` is not read for a zone). A zone with a size not above 0, a number not finite, or axes not unit and square to 1e-6, and an ask with both faces and a zone, is left out and listed (`Outcome::empty_regions`). | the app's "Lattice zones" (LatticeReview's brief C, 2026-11-14; `Zone`, `region_from_zone`, `Settings::RegionAsk::zone`; file job key `region_zones`) |
| D22 | **Every face cut is respected.** A region, and a Protect skin, may be given as PIECES of faces instead of whole faces: a piece is some faces of the model and a list of cuts, a cut a half-space {point, normal, strict} that keeps a point p when (p − point) · normal ≥ 0, or > 0 when strict (core's `FaceRegionSpec` cut, exactly). The surface of a piece is its faces' surface where every cut holds; the region's surface is the union of its pieces' surfaces, and everything the engine does with a region's faces it does with that surface instead: the true outline read off its triangles, the cut edges included (D10); one region, one depth, one outline, no seam between its pieces (D20); a flat surface a prism, anything else a shell; Expand in the plane (D11); a Protect skin's depth under the piece only. The surface is made by cutting the faces' triangles exactly: every triangle of the region's faces is cut by every cut of every one of its pieces, and each part is kept when some piece holding its face keeps it, so two pieces that share a cut, or a cut that ends on another piece, join with no seam and no gap; a triangle no cut crosses is the model's own. With pieces, the faces are not read. A piece with no faces, a face with no triangles, a cut whose normal is zero or whose numbers are not finite, pieces that leave no surface, or pieces asked for together with a zone: the region is left out and listed (`Outcome::empty_regions`); such a Protect skin has no surface (the file job refuses it, `protect_faces_missing`). The cuts stand up out of the face: a point belongs to a Protect skin given as pieces only if it is within the skin's depth of the pieces' surface AND every cut of at least one of its pieces holds at that point (a region's prism or shell already stops at its outline). With no pieces anywhere every layout, bar and file is as before, byte for byte. A point exactly on a cut is held by exactly one of the two pieces it divides, never both and never neither; which one follows the region's own edge rule, not `strict`. Pieces of one region that overlap are kept once. | the owner, 2026-11-14: every face cut must be respected without question (LatticeReview's brief G; `FacePiece`, `FaceCut`, `region_from_pieces`, `pieces_mesh`, `Settings::RegionAsk::pieces`, `Settings::ProtectAsk::pieces`; file job keys `region_pieces`, `protect_pieces`) |
| D23 | **The faces a region runs into that no rule decides are asked about (places).** For each FLAT region made from faces (not the whole part / All lattice, not a curved or joined region, not a zone) whose Finish is not Covered: (1) Reached faces. For every model face that is not one of the region's own faces (or face pieces): sample its triangles on the unwelded model mesh; a sample counts as reached when the point 0.05 mm inside the surface is lattice of THIS region, or is solid ONLY because of a chip's answer (a `protect_chip` entry: it would be lattice of the region with that entry left out). Keep the reached area. (2) Decided by rule, never a place: (a) the face straight opposite: outward normal within 30 degrees of the prism's inward direction over at least half its area (it is latticed through: the owner pushed the prism through it); (b) anything reached only near the prism's own boundary: a sample is DEEP when it lies more than one of the region's own cells inside the prism's side outline AND more than one own cell below the mouth; a face is a SEED only when at least half of its whole area is reached and deep. The owner's own Protect skin (protect_chip 0) stays a rule: no place there. (3) Places. Seeds joined across shared edges are one place. A face that is reached and deep over at least a quarter of its area joins a neighbouring place but never starts one. A place whose seeds' reached area is under 100 mm2 is dropped (the rule keeps it lattice). (4) Each place reports: the region's id; its faces' raw ids (seeds first); a point on the surface (the reached, deep sample nearest the area-weighted centre of the place's reached area); the outward normal there; the reached area in mm2; `now`: "lattice" (what the engine makes there today, read from `where`), or "solid" when most of its reached area is under a chip's protect entry. (5) Same input, same bytes: the order is by region id, then by the lowest raw face id of the place. The answers are not the engine's: "Lattice" sends nothing; "Solid" arrives as a Protect entry on those faces marked `protect_chip` 1, which acts in the layout as any Protect entry (solid to its depth, wins everywhere) and keeps its place listed, so the answer can be changed back. | the owner, 2026-11-17 (to the app agent, on a picture of an old band chip): "Yes, add a chip to ask what it should be"; the lead's design (Install 3, `brief-2-places.md` and its amendment; `find_places`, `Place`, `Outcome::places`, `Settings::ProtectAsk::chip`; file job key `protect_chip`) |

## 2. Cell size

| # | Rule | Status and source |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Largest cells, fewest cells, everywhere. The smallest cells go only where no larger cell fits, and as few of them as possible. | his, 2026-09-14: "Always aim for the largest possible cell size because we want to use as FEW CELLS AS POSSIBLE … The smallest cells go in the areas none of the larger cells can go … and as little as possible." (`LatticeOctreeBake.swift:5`) |
| C2 | A cell is never wider than the wall it sits in. On a 12 mm wall the cell is 12 mm, not 13. The wall is the lattice's own column at that spot: from the face (or a Protect skin's inner side, or where the material begins when a pocket lies under the face) to the far surface, a far skin, or the region's depth. Material beyond a pocket, or beyond the region's depth, is not part of it. | his, 2026-08-24: "I'd rather it never overshoot — that a 13mm cell never be on a 12mm wall; instead have a 12mm cell on the 12mm wall" (`LatticeRegionCells.swift:92`) |
| C3 | Aesthetic with single-cell walls switched off: a wall holds lattice at 2 cells across, always, whatever the stress. | his, 2026-08-21: "always lattice at 2 cells/member max" (`LatticeStageMode.swift:112`); confirmed 2026-11-10 (ruling 1) |
| C4 | Allow single-cell members (Aesthetic only), on unless he switches it off: a wall may hold one cell across, and that cell is the largest that fits. It does not force a finish: Finish None stays allowed (S4), with the warning of U13. A project saved with the switch off keeps it off. | his, 2026-08-22 and 2026-08-26: "single-cell/member means make the largest single cell across the entire model" (`LatticeSettings.swift:2255`, `:2285`); on by default, his, 2026-11-11: "Yes, set single-cell on" |
| C5 | Structural: the engine's certified number of cells across a wall (5 today). Below it the wall is solid. | reviewer, 2026-11-10 |
| C6 | Stepped packs a base cell S with cells of any whole fraction of it, not only halves: every k × (S/n) for n = 2 to 6, plus S itself. A 12 mm cell may go 9 + 3 or 3 + 3 + 3 + 3. Default Grade stays halves (C7), so the two never look the same. | his, 2026-08-26 (`LatticePreviewOccupancy.swift:1040`) and 2026-11-10: "Stepped is ANY denomination. | C6a | A fraction is allowed only if its smallest tile prints open (C14: 5.27 × bead, 2.37 mm at 0.45). At a 0.45 mm bead a 12 mm cell may use halves, thirds, quarters and fifths (2.4 mm); sixths (2.0 mm) are out, and with them 10 + 2. A wider base or a finer bead lets them back in. | reviewer, 2026-10-10 and 2026-11-02 (c) (`stepped_plan.hpp:16`) |
| C6b | A cell at the face leaves the rest of the wall to tiles of the same fraction, which fill it exactly. No solid strip is laid to make up a depth. | his, 2026-09-17 (`stepped_plan.hpp:10`) |
| C6c | One base cell is packed from one family: a smallest tile and the sizes that are whole numbers of it, each started on a multiple of it. Of the families that fit, the one covering most of the space wins, then the one with fewest cells. | reviewer, 2026-10 (`LatticeOctreeBake.swift:1480`); his rule C1 |
| C7 | Default Grade halves: S, S/2, S/4 … down to the printable floor. | his, 2026-10-02, ruling 4 (`LatticeSettings.swift:2366`) |
| C8 | A leftover smaller than the printable floor is a cut piece of the larger cell beside it. It is solid only where that cut piece is itself too small to print. | his, 2026-08-28: "Once you hit the printability floor go solid." (`LatticeSDFMetal.swift:2781`); narrowed by him 2026-11-10 (section 8, item 4) |
| C9 | The layout is centred on the part, so a symmetric part gets a symmetric layout. | proposed |
| C10 | Manual: minimum cells across a wall, cell size per zone or region, layout anchor and angle, and the largest size step between neighbours can each be set. | agreed, 2026-11-10 (Manual controls 2 to 4) |
| C11 | A wall's width is the thickness of the material at that spot, measured along the region's own direction. It is never the region's depth, and a corner where two walls meet is not a thick wall. | his, 2026-08-20 ("the walls here are *exactly* 20mm wide. | C11a | The number of cells a wall must hold (1, 2 or 5 across: C3 to C5) is counted across the wall's NARROWEST width, whichever way that lies. An inside corner, where a step or a rib meets a wall, is not a narrow place. Under a flat face the narrowest width is read once per column, at the column's middle: grooves or a chamfer in a face do not make the wall behind them a narrow member. The count is taken across the ROOM BETWEEN THE SKINS: the narrowest width less what Protect skins or the cover take of that same line, because that is where the cells live (reviewer's ruling, 2026-11-11 23:52Z, which also accepted the once-per-column reading). | reviewer's ruling, 2026-11-11 08:04Z: "the cells-per-member FLOOR, the 1/2/5 across, must use the member's NARROWEST width: the inscribed ball ... | C12 | Stepped: the cell at each spot fits the smaller of the region's depth and the material there, in both directions. | C22 | **Cell size by stress.** With it on, the stress asks for a cell size place by place: the region's own cell where the stress is nothing, the smallest cell its ladder allows where the stress is at or over the reference, in proportion between. **A cell takes the size on its ladder nearest to what the stress asks; the smallest always may.** With d the highest stress in the axis-aligned box round the cube's eight corners (R16), t = (d ÷ reference, held to 0 to 1) to the power of the response (held to 0.25 to 4), the stress asks for S* = S_base − t (S_base − f), f the finest cell, and a cell of size s may stand where S* ≥ (s + s_below) ÷ 2, s_below the next smaller size of its ladder; a tie keeps the larger, and a size with none below always may. The ladder is one sorted set per pass: Default Grade its halves; Stepped the base S and every m × S ÷ n for each admitted fraction n and m = 1 to n − 1 (the sizes it can lay). So at t = 0 the region's own cell, at t = 1 the finest; with two sizes the own cell stands where t ≤ 0.5; base 12 with Stepped admitting 2 and 3 (12, 8, 6, 4): 12 where S* ≥ 10, 8 where S* ≥ 7, 6 where S* ≥ 5. It only ever allows more than the rule before it (S* ≥ s): nothing that was lattice goes solid for it. Known and accepted: inside one fraction's packing the nearest size may not be one that fraction can lay, and the packing then lays its own next smaller size; so a cell is never larger than the ladder size nearest to what the stress asks, and may be smaller (base 12 at S* 9.6 lays 6s: the halves cover the same with fewer cells than an 8 among 4s). A cell the stress does not allow is held exactly as one the band does not allow: divided down its ladder (Default Grade halves, Stepped packs: every cell Stepped tries is tested against the stress as against the band, and with no band its spots are tried quietest first, so the larger cells take the quietest places). With Grade to shape on as well, both rules must allow a cell. Nothing goes solid for the stress (as G6): a cell that cannot be divided further stands. The stress never lays a cell under the smallest the layout would otherwise lay there (the floor, G11 and C14 are untouched) and does not change which cells the outline may cut (G10). The reference is the stated one, else the part's own (R17), computed once before the cells are laid, kept while the part, the field and the percentile are the same, one for the cells and the struts alike, and read only when it is asked for (struts By stress, or cell size by stress that would be applied): a field sent under Uniform is never read for it. **Off, whatever the switch says,** when there is no stress result or it is empty, the reference is 0, a cell size is typed (only Manual forces one size, C16), or the algorithm is Uniform.
