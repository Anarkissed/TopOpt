### 3. squish_envelope (data.cpp:368-372, squish.cpp:92-96)
- **Core call:** `squish_envelope(data, material, temps, family, beads, bead_mm, stack, weight)`. Per column it returns:
  - **ρ_env** = max(the softest TESTED row, the 2-cell floor). The 2-cell floor: the cell must be ≤ min(the column's run, the face's
    smaller extent) / 2, so ρ ≥ c·t / cell_max, with gyroid c = 3.0915 and honeycomb c = 2.
  - **Auto's family:** the topologies Auto may pick; honeycomb is out on a side stack, per R6.
  - **the deepest squish at the column's pressure on ρ_env**, with its zone: tested / extrapolated / densification ESTIMATE.
- **Past the tested strain:** the textbook densification law, joined C¹ at the curve's end and capped at the solid. Gibson & Ashby,
  *Cellular Solids*, 2nd ed., 1997: ε_D ≈ 1 − 1.4 ρ_rel. This is the same law as the app's `Law::lattice_at`
  (flexible_squish_fe.cpp), MOVED into core so the app and core read one source.
- **Applicability, said by name in the receipt:** Gibson & Ashby derived ε_D for open-cell FOAMS. A gyroid sheet and a honeycomb
  pushed along its prisms are not foams, and their densification is untested here. So the law is a MARKED estimate, never a tested
  limit, until rows to densification exist (core brief #40).
- **Red first:**
  - a 23 mm squish through a 25 mm slab (92 %) at ρ 0.143 is past ε_D (0.80): flagged "past densification", not accepted;
  - his pad's faces, against the app's law, to its tolerance.
